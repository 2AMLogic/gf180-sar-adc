# Investigation: is the switching event resolved, and which timestep makes the event charge trustworthy? (issue #386)

- **Date / author**: 2026-10-07, Builder agent (issue #386)
- **Status**: methodology control for `sim/vdd-switching-event/`. **Not an evidence record.**
  It runs one PVT point (`ff_-40c_3.63v`, the corner where `sim/adc-rail-current/` measured
  its grid-worst peak) on a shortened 7.4 µs run, so it cannot substantiate a corner claim.
  What it does is pick the deck's timestep and show that the numbers taken at that timestep
  have converged. The PVT claim is the record under `../records/`.
- **Why this is one point and not 27**: a numerical-convergence question (does the
  integrator resolve the edge, and does `.meas INTEG` converge) is answered at the hardest
  point. That is the point with the fastest, largest edge (ff, −40 °C, 3.63 V). The full grid
  then runs at the chosen setting. Per this host's rules, single-corner probes run locally
  (one `ngspice -b` each, one at a time). The grid ran on the batch fleet.

## Probes

All three decks were generated by `sim/harness/runner.compose_deck` itself
(`20261007-issue-386-probes/mk_probe.py`), so the circuit is byte-for-byte the one
`sim/adc-rail-current/` recorded against. Same netlist (`sim/adc-power/testbench/tb_adc_power.spice`,
sha256 `43fef1c1…c53d1`), same PDK (gf180mcuD, open_pdks `c6d73a35…`), ngspice-46, `set
num_threads=1`. The only changes are the analysis lines and a `wrdata` dump of the summed
`vdd` current. `tstop` is 7.4 µs, which covers conversions 3–7: every event type, plus the
peak at 7.00286 µs.

| probe | analysis | integrator | wall | RSS | points |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `p2n` | `tran 1n 7.4u 0 2n` | trapezoidal (default) | 24 s | 63 MB | 19 200 |
| `pg2n` | `tran 1n 7.4u 0 2n` + `.options method=gear` | Gear | 31 s | 75 MB | 24 633 |
| `p10p` | `tran 1n 7.4u 0 10p` | trapezoidal (default) | 9 min 46 s | 1.7 GB | 744 283 |

Decks, logs and reductions are in `20261007-issue-386-probes/`.
`analyze_probe.py` reduces each probe's `wrdata` dump on its own terms: it applies the
trapezoid rule to the saved points and does not use ngspice's `.meas`. That makes it an
independent check on `.meas INTEG` as well as on the timestep. The 100+ MB waveform dumps
are not committed. Re-running `mk_probe.py` + `ngspice -b` + `analyze_probe.py` regenerates
them.

## Finding 1: the 2 ns cap did resolve the peak

DR-0036 step 2 inferred that the event "cannot have been resolved" by a 2 ns maximum
timestep. The inference was reasonable, but the waveform shows it is wrong. The cap is a
maximum. ngspice's truncation-error control shrinks the step at the edge regardless, and
in `p2n` the accepted steps through the main peak at 7.00286 µs are **7–28 ps** (the
saved points from 7.002740 to 7.002985 µs). The peak moves very little once the cap goes
to 10 ps:

| quantity | `p2n` (2 ns, trap) | `pg2n` (2 ns, Gear) | `p10p` (10 ps, trap) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| peak of summed current, 6.99–7.10 µs (`.meas MIN`) | 34.3839 mA | 34.7373 mA | 34.6950 mA |
| same, `vddd` alone | 34.2987 mA | 34.6395 mA | 34.5967 mA |
| time beyond half of peak, event A (k=7) | 0.0896 ns | 0.0886 ns | 0.0868 ns |

`p2n` reproduces `sim/adc-rail-current/`'s committed value exactly (34.383980 mA at
7.00286 µs), which confirms the probe deck is that deck. At 10 ps the peak is **+0.9 %**
higher. So the earlier record's peak was a lower bound by about 1 %, not by the large
factor the 2 ns cap seemed to imply. The event is about **0.09 ns** wide at half its peak.

## Finding 2: ngspice's `.meas INTEG` is NOT trustworthy at a 2 ns cap

The window integrals are a different matter. Take the same one-bit-cycle windows the record
uses (A: 6.999–7.0615 µs, B: 7.249–7.3115 µs, raw window charge, delivered, pC):

| quantity | `p2n` `.meas INTEG` | `p2n` trapezoid of saved points | `pg2n` `.meas INTEG` | `p10p` `.meas INTEG` | `p10p` trapezoid |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| window A, summed | **6.0690** | 5.5818 | 5.5480 | 5.5634 | 5.5637 |
| window A, `vddd` | **3.8919** | — | 3.3843 | 3.3882 | — |
| window B, summed | **2.3515** | 4.4125 | 4.4063 | 4.3777 | 4.4018 |

At the 2 ns cap, ngspice's own `.meas INTEG` is off by **+9 %** on window A and **−47 %**
on window B. A trapezoid sum over the same run's saved points agrees with every other
column to about 1 %. So the saved waveform is fine. What fails is `.meas INTEG`'s reduction
of it, on the long steps between edges, where trapezoidal integration leaves a
sign-alternating ringing on the source currents (visible in `p2n`'s saved points after
7.003 µs: ±0.5 mA, flipping sign every step).

It is also fragile. Adding a measurement-only `par('i(vddc)+i(vddd)+i(vddt)')` card, which
ngspice implements as an internal B-source on a node of its own, changes 81 of the 126
per-branch window integrals at the 2 ns cap, some by orders of magnitude. The peak and
average do not move (`klt-local-smoke-2ns.json`: the `expr`-form and `par`-form requests
on the same point).

At a 10 ps cap this stops:

- `p10p`'s `.meas INTEG` agrees with the trapezoid reduction of its own points to
  ≤ 0.6 % (A: 5.5634 vs 5.5637; B: 4.3777 vs 4.4018).
- It agrees with the Gear run, whose integrator has no trapezoidal ringing, to ≤ 0.65 %
  (A: 5.5634 vs 5.5480; B: 4.3777 vs 4.4063).
- The baseline-subtracted event charges (`*.reduced.txt`) agree across all three
  integrations to ≤ 1.5 % (A: 3.372 / 3.397 / 3.417 pC; B: 2.210 / 2.255 / 2.248 pC).

**Provisional choice (superseded by Finding 5): `tran 1n 17u 0 10p`, trapezoidal**, i.e. `sim/adc-rail-current/`'s analysis
with only the maximum timestep changed. That keeps the integrator the shared deck uses
and changes exactly one setting. The Gear column is the independent check that this
setting has converged. Gear is not used for the grid because it would change two things
at once.

## Finding 3: the rail absorbs current, and it is not a numerical artefact

`MAX` of the summed current over 3–7.4 µs is positive in all three probes: **+7.90 mA**
(`p2n`), **+6.54 mA** (`pg2n`), **+6.71 mA** (`p10p`), each at 5.87753 µs. Almost all of
it is on `vddd`. That time is 1.25 ns after event C of conversion 5, the ph13→ph14 edge
where `endconv` releases every engaged leg back to V_cm. Under Gear the excursion is one
smooth positive lobe, about 0.15 ns wide (`pg2n`'s saved points, 5.87749–5.87763 µs),
directly after the event's own ~11.7 mA delivery dip. Gear does not ring, so this is
current the circuit pushes into the supply source, not trapezoidal ringing.

DR-0036 step 1 assumed this never happens. The record measures it at every PVT point.

## Finding 4: where the events are

Every excursion of the summed current beyond 3 mA in 3–7.4 µs (`*.reduced.txt`, top
section) falls on one of three array-wide edges of each conversion, at the same offset in
every conversion:

| event | clock edge | offset of peak | peak (`p10p`) |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| A | ph15→ph0, bottom plates V_cm → V_in | t_k + 2.86 ns | 32.8–34.7 mA |
| B | ph3→ph4, bottom plates V_in → V_cm | t_k + 252.85 ns | 25.9–26.8 mA |
| C | ph13→ph14, engaged legs released to V_cm | t_k + 877.44 ns | 11.4–11.7 mA |

At 10 ps, events A and B also show a 3–4 mA after-peak 0.1 ns behind the main one (t_k + 2.95 / 2.97 ns and t_k + 252.93 / 252.95 ns). It is part of the same event and falls inside the same window. The bit trials in between switch one cell per side and stay below 3 mA. The record's deck
integrates one bit-cycle window around each of these three edges in every conversion.

## Finding 5: finer caps abort on the fleet; Gear with a 250 ps cap completes

The first fleet submission used the 10 ps cap chosen above. Every one of its 9 corners
(the `ss` shard of each rail's request) aborted identically, after ~283 s:

    doAnalyses: TRAN:  Timestep too small; time = 7.71885e-06, timestep = 1.25e-23: trouble with node "pa_00"

The same time and node in every corner means a deterministic stall of the model/clocking
at that instant, not corner-dependent stiffness. It is past the 7.4 µs the `p10p` probe
covered, so the probe could not have seen it. Single-corner local probes at the `ss`, 27 °C,
2.97 V point, to 7.8 µs (`20261007-issue-386-probes/pss_25p.*`), and at the `ff`, -40 °C,
3.63 V point, to 7.4 µs (`pff_25p.*`), with a **25 ps** cap:

| quantity | `p10p` (10 ps, ff -40 °C 3.63 V, to 7.4 µs) | `pff_25p` (25 ps, same point) | difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| peak of summed current, 6.99-7.10 µs | 34.6950 mA | 34.9805 mA | +0.8 % |
| window A, summed (`.meas INTEG`) | 5.5634 pC | 5.5643 pC | +0.02 % |
| window A, `vddd` | 3.3882 pC | 3.3938 pC | +0.17 % |
| window B, summed | 4.3777 pC | 4.4049 pC | +0.6 % |

and `pss_25p` (ss, 27 °C, 2.97 V) runs through 7.71885 µs to its 7.8 µs end
(peak 15.70 mA at 7.00289 µs, window A 3.606 pC, window B 2.937 pC; MAX of the summed
current +1.46 mA, the rail absorbing). So 25 ps is within the ~1 % the 10 ps and Gear
runs already agree to, and it completes where 10 ps does not.

A second fleet submission at 25 ps (trapezoidal, 9 shards per rail) got past 7.7 µs and
then aborted in every corner it reached, identically (`Timestep too small; time =
1.53751e-05, timestep = 3.125e-23: trouble with node "pa_00"`). So the stall is a property
of the trapezoidal run at certain instants, not of a particular cap. One local probe on the
`ss`, 27 °C, 2.97 V point to 15.6 µs with `.options method=gear` and the same 25 ps cap
(`pss_g25_15.*`) completes:

| quantity (ss 27 °C 2.97 V) | trap 25 ps (`pss_25p`, to 7.8 µs) | Gear 25 ps (`pss_g25_15`, to 15.6 µs) |
|---|---:|---:|
| window A, summed | 3.6064 pC | 3.6079 pC |
| window A, `vddd` | 1.7948 pC | 1.7984 pC |
| window B, summed | 2.9374 pC | 2.9377 pC |
| peak of summed current, 6.99-7.10 µs | 15.697 mA | 15.123 mA |
| MAX of summed current, 3 µs to end | +1.46 mA | +1.93 mA |

Window charges agree to <= 0.2 %. The peak does not: Gear is 3.7 % below trapezoidal here
(at the ff point, Gear at 2 ns was 1.0 % above trapezoidal at 2 ns). The peak of a ~0.09 ns
event is integrator-dependent at the few-percent level and the record states it that way;
the charge, which is what DR-0036's budget uses, is not.

Gear alone is not enough. A third fleet submission (Gear, 25 ps, 3 shards of 3 corners)
aborted in every `ff` corner at the same instant, 15.37751 µs (`Timestep too small ... node
"vddc#branch"` / `"pa_00"`), while its `tt` and `ss` shards exceeded the fleet's 3600 s job
limit (concurrency-1 instance, three ~8-minute-locally corners each) and returned nothing.
The failing instant in all of these is a clock edge (7.71885 µs = 123.5 periods + 0.1 ns,
15.37751 µs = 246 periods + 0.1 ns), and it moves with cap and integrator, which is what a
Newton non-convergence at an edge looks like rather than a model singularity. One local
probe on the `ff`, -40 °C, 2.97 V point to 15.5 µs with `.options method=gear itl4=100`
and a 25 ps cap (`pff_g25_itl.*`) completes (peak 23.34 mA at 7.00286 µs; window A
4.092 pC; window B 3.370 pC; MAX of the summed current +4.56 mA).

A fourth fleet submission with `.options method=gear itl4=100` and the 25 ps cap failed
the same way, in every corner of the rail (all 9, after ~200 s, at 15.37751 µs), even though
the local probes at the same settings completed. A local probe with the fleet's `.meas
par(...)` cards in place of the harness's `let` (`pff_par.*`) also ran through 15.5 µs, so the
`par()` B-source is not the cause. The cause is not isolated here: the stall is at a clock
edge, moves with cap and integrator, and appears on the fleet but not on this host, which is
what a borderline Newton step at an edge looks like when two machines' floating-point paths
differ. That makes any very fine cap fragile on this deck.

The cap is therefore a trade, not a free choice, and the evidence on it is:

| cap | integrator | where | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 ns | trapezoidal | `sim/adc-rail-current/` (committed) | completes, all 27 points; `.meas INTEG` off by +9 % / -47 % |
| 2 ns | Gear | local probe `pg2n` | completes to 7.4 µs; charges within 0.6 % of 10 ps |
| 10 ps | trapezoidal | local `p10p` / fleet | completes to 7.4 µs locally; fleet aborts at 7.71885 µs |
| 25 ps | trapezoidal | local `pss_25p`, `pff_25p` / fleet | completes to 7.8 / 7.4 µs locally; fleet aborts at 15.3775 µs |
| 25 ps | Gear + itl4=100 | local `pss_g25_15`, `pff_g25_itl` / fleet | completes to 15.5-15.6 µs locally; fleet aborts at 15.3775 µs |
| **250 ps** | **Gear + itl4=100** | **fleet** | **completes (record)** |

**Choice: `tran 1n 17u 0 250p` with `.options method=gear itl4=100`.** 250 ps is far above
the ~0.09 ns half-width of the event, but Finding 1 already showed that the cap is only a
ceiling: ngspice's truncation-error control takes the 7-28 ps steps through the edge itself.
What Gear + a 250 ps cap fixes is the long steps between edges, which is where the
trapezoidal `.meas INTEG` went wrong (Finding 2). The record states the peak's
integrator dependence (Finding 5 table: -3.7 % to +1.0 % between Gear and trapezoidal at
different points) and carries the charge, which agrees across integrators to <= 0.6 %, as
the primary result. The 10 ps and 25 ps failures are not root-caused here (reported in the
PR).

## Cross-check of the fleet run (addendum, written after the record was minted)

The record's fleet run (`tran 1n 17u 0 250p`, Gear, itl4=100) at `ff_-40c_3.63v`, conversion 7,
against the 10 ps trapezoidal probe `p10p` of the same point and window:

| quantity | `p10p` (10 ps, trap, local) | record (250 ps, Gear, fleet) | difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| peak of summed current, event A7 | 34.6950 mA | 34.7464 mA | +0.15 % |
| window A, summed | 5.5634 pC | 5.5484 pC | -0.27 % |
| window A, `vddd` | 3.3882 pC | 3.3835 pC | -0.14 % |
| window B, summed | 4.3777 pC | 4.4051 pC | +0.63 % |

The fleet's `klt` form of the deck (wrapper body, `par()` peak cards, `save all`) reproduces the
local harness form to the ~1 % the probes agree to among themselves. The probe-versus-fleet
comparison is one PVT point and one conversion; it supports the setting, it does not certify
the other 26 points. The mid-run `Timestep too small` aborts at finer caps remain unexplained.
