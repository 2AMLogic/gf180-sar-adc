- **Note**: PVT SUBSET, STATED RATHER THAN HIDDEN: this deck contains no PDK device models at all. It is rung 1 of DR-0010's fidelity ladder -- ideal XSPICE event-driven digital primitives (d_dff/d_and/d_or/d_inverter), ideal bridges, and behavioural analog -- so the process and temperature axes cannot move any number in it, and sweeping them would manufacture the appearance of a PVT campaign that carries no information. - **Note**: The behavioural CDAC in this deck is built FROM THE CONTROLLER'S OWN switch-driver outputs (each cell's bottom plate takes V_in, V_cm, V_REF or GND according to the sel_in/rel/sel_hi/sel_lo gate drives the DUT actually asserts), not from an independent model of what the controller should have done. That is what makes the closed loop a test rather than a tautology: a wrong direction, a wrong side, a cell that engages a trial early or never releases all move the top plate wrongly and produce a wrong code. Under DR-0014 the top plate is 2*V_cm - V_sampled + DAC, so the sampled input enters inverted -- which is why the controller inverts the comparator once at its boundary, and why a loop that got only ONE of those two inversions right would either diverge or emit the one's complement of every code.
- **Note**: What this record does NOT claim: nothing here is evidence about gf180mcu gate delay, about real CDAC settling (sim/cdac-bit-settling/ owns that; the 2.56 ns first-order lag in this deck is a placeholder from spec/prior-art-survey.md Sec 1.4, not a measurement), about comparator behaviour (#9), or about power. It is evidence that the sequencing, the switch decode and the output register are functionally correct on every code in both input modes.
- **Note**: WHAT THE TWO-PHASE SAMPLE CHECK COVERS AND WHAT IT DOES NOT. iso_gap_ns is a property of the controller's own two output controls, and its sign is the ordering claim. It is NOT evidence that the resulting charge transfer is correct at transistor level: the four things DR-0014's Consequences require -- the top-plate switch's own injection and its side-to-side mismatch, the bottom-plate legs' injection after that switch has opened, the fourth leg's effect on bit-trial settling, and second-order residue from C_par mismatch -- are all analog measurements on the transistor-level converter, and #61 owns them.
- **Note**: WHY THE TWO-PHASE SAMPLE IS MEASURED AS A DUTY CYCLE AND NOT AS AN EDGE PAIR, and why its tight bound lives in sim/sar-logic-timing/. The first version of this check measured the two controls' falling edges directly (meas WHEN v(...)=1.4 FALL=k) and required their difference to be one bit cycle to within 1 ns. This deck cannot resolve that: 1024.5 us at a 20 ns maximum timestep means a threshold crossing on a 0.3 ns bridge transition is interpolated from samples up to 20 ns apart. Record 20260802-094246-16ec0f1 -- committed, failing -- measured this same unchanged design as 61.28 ns at conversion 5 and 63.30 ns at conversion 500, a 2 ns spread on a quantity whose true value is one clock plus one inverter. That is the instrument's resolution, not the design's jitter. The fix was to measure something the instrument CAN resolve (a time average over whole conversions is an integral, so it does not care where the samples land inside an edge, and it is phase-independent) and to put the tight bound in the 8.5 us / 5 ns deck that can carry it. Widening the window until the unresolved number fitted would have been the other option, and it is the one this repo does not take.
- **Note**: Supersedes 20260802-094246-16ec0f1, which is a CHECK correction and not a design correction: that record was taken on this same DR-0014 controller and every functional number in it is identical to this one. What changed is the two-phase-sample instrument. Its first form measured the two sample controls falling edges with meas WHEN, which this deck cannot resolve -- 1024.5 us at a 20 ns maximum timestep interpolates a 0.3 ns bridge transition from samples up to 20 ns apart, and the superseded record accordingly read the same design as 61.28 ns at conversion 5 and 63.30 ns at conversion 500. Both quantities are now duty cycles averaged over 1000 whole conversions, which is an integral and therefore timestep-insensitive; the tight bounds on them are sim/sar-logic-timing/ (8.5 us at 5 ns), and the bounds here are a +/-2.5 ns regression guard against a whole clock appearing or disappearing.
