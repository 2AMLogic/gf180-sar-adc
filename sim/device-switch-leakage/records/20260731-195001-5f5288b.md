- **Note**: JUNCTION LEAKAGE IS NOT MODELLED. Branch 2 is a null control: the same device with 1000 um^2 of declared junction area (as = ad = 1e-9 m^2, ps = pd = 1e-3 m, ~100x any plausible diffusion) leaks identically to the zero-area default, at every temperature. The gf180mcu 3.3 V FET model cards define Cj/Cjsw/Pb but no JS/JSW/JSWG (audited independently by sim/tools/pdk_mismatch_audit.py, finding `mos-junction-leakage`). Consequence for #8/#10: the 125 C hold-droop numbers here are a LOWER BOUND -- they contain subthreshold channel leakage only, and real drain-body junction leakage must be budgeted from foundry data or literature, not from this PDK.
- **Note**: Because junction geometry provably has no effect in this PDK, branches 3-7 do not declare as/ad/ps/pd. Declaring it would imply a precision the models do not have.
- **Note**: NUMERICAL FLOOR, MEASURED. ngspice's default gmin (1e-12 S) would put a ~1.6 pA floor across every junction at mid-rail and swamp every number here, so this testbench sets gmin = 1e-18 with abstol = 1e-21. A residual floor of ~10 fA remains, and branch 7 measures it instead of asserting it: a 20/2 PMOS in the identical off-state bias reads the same current as the 20/0.28 device, at every temperature and process corner, which no real channel-leakage mechanism could do. pmos_floor_ratio checks that. Readings at or below ~10 fA are therefore UPPER BOUNDS, not measurements -- the whole PMOS branch, and the -40 C column generally, are in that regime. The 125 C NMOS and T-gate columns, which are what a hold-droop budget is actually built on, sit 4-5 orders of magnitude above the floor and are real.
- **Note**: T-GATE SIGN AND COLD-CORNER CANCELLATION. ileak_tg_signed_fa keeps the sign that ileak_tg_fa discards. Across this grid the net is single-signed (no zero crossing was observed), but its MAGNITUDE collapses at the cold corners because the PMOS half cancels most of the NMOS half: at ss/-40 C the net residual is 3.7e-4 fA while each half individually reads the deck's ~10 fA floor, i.e. the floor mechanism cancels between the two halves almost exactly. A sub-fA residual of two floor-level currents is not a measurement of anything, so no lower bound is asserted on the T-gate magnitude — a floor there would fail on a physically correct cancellation. At 125 C the NMOS half dominates by orders of magnitude and the net is a real, budgetable current; that is the only column a hold-droop budget should use.
- **Note**: Gate leakage is not separately reported. These are 3.3 V thick-oxide devices where gate tunnelling is negligible next to junction and subthreshold leakage, and the PDK's BSIM cards do not enable a gate-current model.
