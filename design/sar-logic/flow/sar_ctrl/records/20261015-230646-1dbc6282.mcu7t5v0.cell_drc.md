## Classification (issue #520)

**Verdict: deck over-approximation (artifact of klt's flat 0.16 um threshold), not
a defect in the P&R flow.** Evidence:

1. The two rules fire on the PDK `endcap` and `filltie` cells *alone*, 4 markers
   per rule per cell (the four sides of a ring), at the same ring geometry the
   macro markers show. `fill_1`/`fill_2` are clean alone (see below).
2. Measured geometry of the isolated cells (1 nm dbu extract, `endcap`): the
   substrate tap is COMP (280,280)-(800,1280) nm, wholly inside a Pplus polygon
   (200,200)-(880,1360) nm, so Pplus extends **0.08 um** past PCOMP; the single
   Nplus polygon sits exactly **0.08 um** from that PCOMP. A threshold sweep
   (`separation_check`/`enclosed_check` on COMP&Pplus) gives 0 violations at
   80 nm and 4 at 81 nm, so the markers are exactly the 0.08 um ring. `filltie`
   is the same with a 840 nm wide COMP.
3. That tap's top edge (y = 1280 nm) is 0.48 um from the cell's Nwell edge
   (y = 1760 nm), i.e. beyond 0.43 um. The klt deck's own documented
   approximation (`klayout_tools/decks/gf180mcu.py`, notes on
   `nplus.space.pcomp.1` / `pplus.enclosing.comp.1`) says the official
   `NP.3b/3c` and `PP.5c/5d` splits relax these to 0.08 um (resp. as little as
   0.02 um) for a tap farther than 0.43 um from a well edge, and that klt
   applies the strict 0.16 um everywhere because it cannot evaluate that
   well-context boolean. The cells' 0.08 um is inside the relaxed band.
4. The macro's markers are by construction these same cells' rings: 208 =
   144 (`endcap`) + 42 (`filltie`) + 20 (`fill_1`) + 2 (`fill_2`) by
   `source_cell`. The `fill_*` markers do not reproduce in isolation; they sit
   at the macro's left/right row ends (x ~ 99.9 / 198 um), coincident with the
   same 0.08 um-wide ring-strip signature (macro dbu 0.5 nm: 160-dbu-wide strips)
   as the abutting tie cell, so they are attributed to the fill cell where it
   abuts a tie/endcap. I did not separately prove the abutting-neighbour
   mechanism with an isolated two-cell placement; treat that attribution as
   likely, not proven.
